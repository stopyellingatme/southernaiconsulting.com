# Southern AI Consulting — brand assets

Everything here is generated by `tools/make_brand_assets.py` and
`tools/make_brand_pdfs.py`, from the letterform in `tools/mark_geometry.py`.
Don't hand-edit these files; change the scripts and re-run them, or the next
regeneration will silently overwrite your edits.

**The wordmark is converted to vector outlines in every file.** Nothing here
depends on the Georgia font being installed, so a print shop cannot accidentally
substitute a different typeface. This is the single most common way a logo comes
back wrong from a printer.

**The mark is one shape in one colour.** It is an S with a circuit trace
running through it: a stroked spine, where the spine is 270° of an ellipse
followed by that same arc turned 180°, and the trace is the letter's own
centreline drawn thin, with five nodes on it.

**The circuit is a hole, not a second colour.** The channel and the rings
around the nodes are cut out of the letter, so what shows through them is
whatever the mark is sitting on — the paper, the card stock, the screen. That
is why every file here works on any background, why there is still only one
ink to print, and why a one-plate job loses nothing at all. It is one path.

**Do not fill the channel in.** If a vendor tells you the logo has "a gap in
it" or offers to "clean it up", that is the design. The only thing to check is
that they honour the even-odd fill rule, which every printer's software does by
default.

**Below about 32 px, use the solid version.** The channel is fine, and under
that it closes up and the letter starts to look smudged rather than detailed.
`logo-mark-solid-*` is the same drawing with the circuit left off — not a
redraw — and it is also what you want for anything that physically cannot hold
a hole: embroidery, a rubber stamp, engraving.

Six earlier versions of this mark exist and are all out of date: a thin
monoline S with three navy dots on it, a single heavy copper stroke with no
dots, a rounded serif S with ball terminals and a bead, a chiselled
high-contrast serif S with three nodes, a block S with its corners cut off at
45°, and a rounded block S with semicircular ends. If you have artwork with any
of them, replace it with these files.

Every file is **tight-cropped to its ink** — no padding is baked in, so the asset
centres correctly when dropped into a layout. Apply clear space yourself (below).

## Which file do I send?

| Situation | Send this |
|---|---|
| Vistaprint / Moo / Canva / any web uploader | `logo-horizontal-colour-4in-300dpi.png` |
| A local print shop or a designer | `logo-horizontal-colour.pdf` (or the `.svg`) |
| Card printed on navy or any dark stock | the `-white` version |
| One colour, foil stamp, letterpress | `logo-horizontal-black.pdf` or `-navy` |
| Embroidery, a rubber stamp, engraving | `logo-mark-solid-black.pdf` — no hole to hold |
| Signage, or the mark on its own anywhere | `logo-mark-*` |
| Anything where the space is square-ish | `logo-stacked-*` |
| Website, email signature, slides | the `.svg` |

If a vendor asks for "vector" or "AI/EPS", send the **PDF** — it is true vector
and every print shop accepts it. If they insist on `.ai` or `.eps`, they can
convert the PDF in seconds; don't let them re-draw it.

## Lockups

- **`logo-horizontal-*`** — mark left, two-line wordmark right. The default. Use this unless the space is narrow.
- **`logo-stacked-*`** — mark centred above the wordmark. For square-ish spaces.
- **`logo-mark-*`** — the S alone. Only where "Southern AI Consulting" already appears in words nearby.
- **`logo-mark-solid-*`** — the S alone, without the circuit. For small sizes and for processes that cannot hold a hole.

## Colourways

- **`-colour`** — copper mark, navy wordmark. The primary. Use on white or the warm off-white.
- **`-navy`** — everything navy. For when copper isn't available or clashes.
- **`-black`** — everything black. Single-plate printing, faxes, newspaper, stamps.
- **`-white`** — everything white. **Reverse only** — on navy, on photos, on dark stock.

The mark is a single ink in all four. The only thing that changes between
`-colour` and the rest is whether the wordmark is a different colour from the
mark, so a one-plate job loses nothing at all — the circuit is a hole, not a
second colour, so there is nothing to drop.

On a navy field where the mark appears **without** the wordmark (the
link-preview card, a slide), set it in the lifted copper `#D99A6C` rather than
the off-white. Off-white is already carrying the type on those layouts, so an
off-white mark reads as another line of the same text block; copper reads as
the logo, and it is the only warm note on the card.

## Colour values

**The hex values are authoritative.** Ask your printer to colour-match to these,
or to pull the closest Pantone from them.

| Colour | Hex | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Ink navy | `#10233A` | the wordmark |
| Copper | `#B26B3F` | the mark |
| Lifted copper | `#D99A6C` | the mark on a navy field — flat copper is only 1.9:1 there and goes muddy |
| Warm off-white | `#FBF9F6` | backgrounds (usually just unprinted paper), and the wordmark when reversed |

The CMYK figures below are a plain unmanaged RGB→CMYK conversion. They are a
**starting point for conversation with your printer, not a press-ready build** —
naive conversion tends to come back muddy on coated stock. Ask them to match the
hex and show you a proof.

| Colour | Approx. CMYK |
|---|---|
| Ink navy | C72 M40 Y0 K77 |
| Copper | C0 M40 Y65 K30 |

## Minimum sizes

Measured, not guessed — below these the wordmark stops being readable.

| Asset | Minimum printed | Minimum on screen |
|---|---|---|
| Horizontal lockup | 1 in wide | 96 px wide |
| Stacked lockup | 0.75 in wide | 72 px wide |
| Mark, with the circuit | 0.2 in tall | 32 px tall |
| Mark, solid | 0.14 in tall | 9 px tall |

The lockup figures are set by the wordmark, which is what stops being readable
first. The mark is taller than it is wide, so its figure is a height.

The circuit's screen floor was measured on a real 1× display at 64, 48, 40, 35,
32, 29, 26, 22, 18, 14, 11, 9 and 7 px of ink. It is crisp to 32, soft at 26 and
closed by 22 — and once it closes it does not simply disappear, it makes the
letter look grubby.

In print the limit is not resolution, it is ink spread. The channel is 4% of the
mark's height, so at 0.2 in tall it is about 0.2 mm wide — around the finest gap
an ordinary press will hold open. Below that, ask for the solid version rather
than hoping. The solid mark has no fine detail at all: its thinnest ink is a
fifth of its height, which is why its own floor is smaller.

The solid mark goes very small: it holds its shape to about 9 px and is still
identifiable at 7. For comparison, the rounded block version it replaced also
managed 9, the chamfered one before that 10, and the serif before that needed
14 px.

Below 9 px use the navy badge (`favicon.svg`) rather than the bare mark — a
filled tile survives where a letter stops.

## Clear space

Keep empty space on all four sides equal to **20% of the logo's height**. Nothing
— no text, rule, edge, or photo — inside that margin.

## Don't

- Don't stretch it. Scale proportionally only.
- Don't recolour it outside the four supplied colourways.
- Don't put the `-colour` or `-navy` version on a dark background; use `-white`.
- Don't add effects — no shadow, glow, outline, bevel, or gradient.
- Don't rebuild the wordmark by typing it in Georgia. Use these files; the spacing here is deliberate.
- Don't crop the mark, and don't try to colour part of it. It is one shape in one ink.
- Don't fill in the circuit, and don't colour it. It is a hole; the background belongs in it.
- Don't use the circuit version below about 32 px on screen. Use `logo-mark-solid-*`.
- Don't outline it or add a keyline. If it needs separation from a busy background, use the badge.

## Business cards

US standard is **3.5 × 2 in**. Ask for:

- **0.125 in bleed** on all sides (so 3.75 × 2.25 in artwork)
- all text and the logo kept **0.125 in inside the trim line**
- **300 dpi minimum** for anything raster
- matte or uncoated stock suits this palette better than high gloss

Suggested front: horizontal lockup at **1.25–1.5 in wide**, upper-left or centred.
If the card is navy, use `logo-horizontal-white`. Leave the copper as the only
warm accent — it does more work when it's the only one.

Name, title, `admin@southernaiconsulting.com`, and
`southernaiconsulting.com` are enough. A phone number is deliberately not on the
website; decide separately whether you want one on the card.
